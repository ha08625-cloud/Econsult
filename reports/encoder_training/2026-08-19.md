# Multi-symptom recombinations: the companion run

*Write-up of the two-arm companion run on 2026-08-19 (ticket 6, task 7). Every
number here comes from the six `<signal>.companion_comparison.json` files in
this folder. **Those files are the authority** — where this document disagrees
with one of them, the JSON is right and this is stale. The scored threshold is
reproducible with `python -m scripts.encoder_training score-companions`.*

**Run:** `roberta-base`, joint six-head, every layer unfrozen, 3 epochs, five
folds over fragment clusters, `GENERATOR_VERSION` 3. Two merged `joint6` trees
generated from the same libraries at the same seed, counts, fold triple and
salt, differing in `--companion-share` and in nothing else: **Arm 0** at 0.0
(44,680 train examples per fold) and **Arm P** at 0.5 (54,410). **Arm C** is Arm
0's trained heads with every margin re-selected on Arm P's validation split — no
retraining, no third dataset. Scored on the fold-pooled synthetic test set and
on the 67 hand-written realistic submissions.

---

## Answer

**It worked, it worked by a lot, and it cost real detection on one signal.**

Across the 402 real-text answers, Arm P asserts a symptom the submission never
mentioned **35 times against Arm 0's 191**. That is the failure this ticket
exists to fix, and it is 82% gone. Arm P is also the first model in this project
to beat the all-`null` floor on real text: **81.0% against 66.7%**, where Arm 0
manages 36.5%.

The cost is 13 decisive cells of 134 — and **8 of those 13 are
`urinary_frequency`**, whose real-text recall on the submissions that do report
it falls from 71.5% to 41.5%. Everywhere else the detection loss is one or two
submissions and inside the noise of a 9-to-18-cell slice.

Arm C captured **16%** of Arm P's mean gain. The expensive half of the ticket
was necessary; the cheap fix alone was not enough.

## 1. The gate, before any score

DD5's leak detector (task 7 instruction 7): mean companions per example must not
differ by label mode, or companion count has become a proxy for the answer and
the run is void rather than reinterpretable.

| arm | share | train examples | companions/example | worst label-mode spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arm 0 | 0.0 | 44,680 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| Arm P | 0.5 | 54,410 | 0.757 | **0.024** |

**Passed.** 0.024 against a mean of 0.757 is the same figure task 6 measured at
generation time, on the same splits, and well inside the 0.05 limit. The
companion polarity check (17/16/67 true/false/null inside `true`, `false` and
`null` primaries alike) was cleared at generation and is recorded in
`arch_training.md` section 10.

## 2. The declared threshold, scored

The four criteria were written into the report headers before the run
(`__main__.COMPANION_PREDICTIONS`), per the house rule that a ceiling asserted
after a disappointing number is an excuse. Scored exactly as written, before any
interpretation:

| criterion | result |
|---|---|
| **Primary** — real-text `null → true` at least 20 points below Arm 0 on ≥4 of 6 signals | **HELD.** 5 of 6. |
| **Guard** — Arm P's real-text accuracy not below Arm 0's | **HELD.** 81.0% against 36.5%. |
| **Negative control** — synthetic `null → true` moves by <2 points | **HELD.** Largest movement 0.60 points. |
| **Arm C** — captures a meaningful fraction of Arm P's gain | **16%.** Meaningful, and small. |
| *(fifth prediction)* the nocturia / urinary frequency pair resists | **Half held.** See section 6. |

### 2.1 Primary

Real-text `null → true`: how often the model answered `true` about a signal the
submission never mentioned. Rates are the mean over five folds; the cell counts
beside them are the rate against that signal's `null` support in the 67
submissions, and are what the rate is actually made of.

| signal | Arm 0 | Arm P | points | `null` cells | invented: Arm 0 → Arm P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fever` | 84.1% | **4.5%** | +79.6 | 49 | ~41 → ~2 |
| `flank_pain` | 87.5% | **17.7%** | +69.8 | 53 | ~46 → ~9 |
| `haematuria` | 80.4% | **12.5%** | +67.9 | 56 | ~45 → ~7 |
| `nocturia` | 69.0% | **19.7%** | +49.3 | 58 | ~40 → ~11 |
| `dysuria` | 72.7% | **23.6%** | +49.1 | 11 | ~8 → ~3 |
| `urinary_frequency` | 25.4% | 6.8% | +18.5 | 41 | ~10 → ~3 |
| **total** | | | | **268** | **~191 → ~35** |

Five of six cleared 20 points against a requirement of four. Two qualifications
belong beside that, neither of which changes the verdict:

**`dysuria`'s pass rests on 11 cells.** Fifty-six of the 67 submissions report
dysuria, so its `null` slice is the smallest in the set and its +49.1 is roughly
8 cells becoming 3. It is a real pass and it is the weakest-supported of the
five. The other four passes sit on 49–58 cells each.

**`urinary_frequency` had the least room.** Its Arm 0 baseline is 25.4%, not the
47% on file from 2026-08-17 — Arm 0 is a regenerated control at
`GENERATOR_VERSION` 3 and DD16 makes every prior number non-comparable. It is
also the only signal to miss the bar, and section 5 shows it is where the whole
detection cost landed.

### 2.2 Guard

Overall real-text accuracy across all 402 answers: **Arm 0 36.5%, Arm C 37.5%,
Arm P 81.0%.** The all-`null` floor is 66.7%.

The guard held by 44.5 points, and **the guard is not the independent check it
was written to be.** 268 of the 402 cells are `null`, so the same cells drive
the guard and the primary criterion; an arm that improved `null` handling and
destroyed everything else could still clear it. That is exactly why section 5
exists, and section 5 — not the guard — is what rules the collapse out.

Clearing the 66.7% floor was explicitly *not* the success condition and no arm
was expected to manage it. Arm P clears it by 14.3 points. Every signal's
real-text accuracy improved: fever 29.0% → 90.5%, haematuria 21.5% → 86.6%,
flank pain 23.0% → 80.3%, nocturia 23.0% → 77.9%, dysuria 72.6% → 78.5%,
urinary frequency 50.1% → 72.2%.

### 2.3 Negative control, read the right way round

Synthetic `null → true`, fold-pooled, ruled view:

| signal | Arm 0 | Arm P | points |
|---|---|---|---|
| `dysuria` | 0.84% | 0.77% | +0.1 |
| `fever` | 1.70% | 1.45% | +0.3 |
| `flank_pain` | 0.28% | 0.84% | −0.6 |
| `haematuria` | 2.27% | 2.26% | +0.0 |
| `nocturia` | 2.61% | 2.97% | −0.4 |
| `urinary_frequency` | 2.44% | 1.84% | +0.6 |

Largest movement 0.60 points against a 2-point limit, in both directions across
the six. **This is the control passing, and a large gain here would have been
the bad outcome**: the synthetic test set cannot contain the failure companions
were built to fix, so a big move would have meant a new shortcut rather than a
removed one. Nothing moved. The synthetic set is as blind to this as it was
predicted to be, which is the second time on file that the 67 submissions have
seen something no amount of generated data could.

### 2.4 Arm C

Arm 0's trained heads, every margin re-selected on Arm P's validation split.
Same weights, same raw argmax scores, same test examples; only the threshold
moves.

| signal | Arm 0 | Arm C | Arm P | C points | P points | captured |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `nocturia` | 69.0% | 53.4% | 19.7% | +15.5 | +49.3 | 31% |
| `urinary_frequency` | 25.4% | 20.5% | 6.8% | +4.9 | +18.5 | 26% |
| `haematuria` | 80.4% | 65.7% | 12.5% | +14.6 | +67.9 | 22% |
| `fever` | 84.1% | 77.6% | 4.5% | +6.5 | +79.6 | 8% |
| `dysuria` | 72.7% | 70.9% | 23.6% | +1.8 | +49.1 | 4% |
| `flank_pain` | 87.5% | 85.3% | 17.7% | +2.3 | +69.8 | 3% |

**Mean capture 16%, and Arm C's overall real-text accuracy is 37.5% against Arm
0's 36.5%** — a point. So the answer to the question Arm C exists to ask is:
the training-data change did the work, and margin re-selection alone would not
have been a substitute. Had this come back near 100% the headline of this ticket
would have been that we spent two arms of GPU time on something a threshold
change already had.

The sub-pattern is worth recording: **the two signals where Arm C did most
(nocturia 31%, urinary frequency 26%) are the two where Arm P did least.** Where
companions moved the head a long way, the margin had little left to do; where
they moved it least, the threshold was still carrying some of the load. Untested
as a mechanism.

## 3. The paired real-text comparison

The 67 submissions are the same for every arm and pair exactly. McNemar per
fold, never pooled — five folds are five models scored on one sample of 67, and
concatenating would report a p-value for 335 observations that do not exist.

| signal | folds Arm 0 better | folds Arm P better | discordant per fold | p per fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `fever` | 0 | 5 | 51/50/49/42/40 | 0.00 × 5 |
| `flank_pain` | 0 | 5 | 43/42/41/44/42 | 0.00 × 5 |
| `haematuria` | 0 | 5 | 52/50/43/50/45 | 0.00 × 5 |
| `nocturia` | 0 | 5 | 43/46/43/29/43 | 0.00 × 5 |
| `urinary_frequency` | 0 | 5 | 40/27/46/21/40 | 0.00/0.02/0.05/0.66/0.02 |
| `dysuria` | 0 | 4 | 10/10/17/17/20 | 1.00/0.00/1.00/0.14/0.82 |

**Arm 0 is better on zero folds of thirty.** On the four strong signals the
discordant counts run 40–52 per fold out of 67 cells — an enormous fraction of
the set changing answer, all of it one way.

**Read the p-values as description, not inference.** They are neither
independent of each other (five overlapping training sets, one sample) nor a
statement about new patients. The discordant counts are the honest measure, and
on `dysuria` they say what the p-values say: 10–20 cells moving, mixed, one fold
a tie. `dysuria` is the weak result in this table and it is the same 11-cell
slice as in section 2.1.

## 4. Where the gain came from, cell by cell

The 402 real-text cells decompose cleanly, and the decomposition is the whole
finding. Cell counts are derived from each rate against its support and
reproduce the reported 36.5% → 81.0% to within rounding.

| | Arm 0 correct | Arm P correct | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`null` cells** (268) | ~35 | ~227 | **+192** |
| **decisive cells** (134) | ~112 | ~98 | **−13** |
| total (402) | ~147 | ~326 | +179 (+44.5 points) |

**Effectively all of the gain is the `null` side, and effectively all of the
loss is one signal.** This is what the ticket predicted it would be doing and it
is worth stating that the prediction was about a mechanism, not a magnitude: the
mechanism is confirmed, the magnitude was not forecast and is larger than
anything in the plan anticipated.

## 5. Did Arm P win by going quiet?

Neither declared criterion can tell "stopped inventing symptoms" apart from
"stopped answering", and this is the check that decides whether the headline is
worth anything. An arm answering `null` to everything would score 0% on the
`null → true` cell, clear the primary criterion on all six signals, and clear
the guard as well — because the guard compares against Arm 0, and Arm 0 sits 30
points below the all-`null` floor.

Real-text accuracy on the **decisive cells alone**, beside per-class recall:

| signal | arm | decisive acc | recall `true` | recall `false` | recall `null` | t/f/n |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dysuria` | Arm 0 | 82.9% | 82.9% | — | 20.0% | 56/0/11 |
| | **Arm P** | 81.1% | 81.1% | — | **65.5%** | |
| `fever` | Arm 0 | 84.4% | 86.7% | 82.2% | 8.6% | 9/9/49 |
| | **Arm P** | 83.3% | 88.9% | 77.8% | **93.1%** | |
| `flank_pain` | Arm 0 | 90.0% | 94.3% | 85.7% | 5.3% | 7/7/53 |
| | **Arm P** | 81.4% | 80.0% | 82.9% | **80.0%** | |
| `haematuria` | Arm 0 | 100.0% | 100.0% | 100.0% | 6.1% | 9/2/56 |
| | **Arm P** | 81.8% | 82.2% | 80.0% | **87.5%** | |
| `nocturia` | Arm 0 | 86.7% | 86.7% | — | 13.1% | 9/0/58 |
| | **Arm P** | 75.6% | 75.6% | — | **78.3%** | |
| `urinary_frequency` | Arm 0 | 71.5% | 71.5% | — | 36.6% | 26/0/41 |
| | **Arm P** | 41.5% | 41.5% | — | **91.7%** | |

**No collapse.** `null` recall rises from 5–37% to 65–92% on every signal while
decisive accuracy stays in the 75–83% band on five of six. Arm P is still
answering; it has stopped answering `true` by default.

**The decisive column has to be read against its cell counts, which are tiny.**
Three of these slices hold 9, 11 and 14 cells:

* `haematuria` 100% → 81.8% is **11 cells, of which 2 changed**. An arm being at
  11/11 was never a stable number.
* `nocturia` 86.7% → 75.6% is 9 cells, **one** of them.
* `flank_pain` 90.0% → 81.4% is 14 cells, **about one**.
* `fever` 84.4% → 83.3% is 18 cells, **about 0.2** — noise in the fold mean.
* `dysuria` 82.9% → 81.1% is 56 cells, about **one**.
* **`urinary_frequency` 71.5% → 41.5% is 26 cells, about 8 of them.** This is
  the only detection loss in the run backed by more than a couple of
  submissions, and it is the largest single movement in the table.

Where a signal has no `false` example anywhere in the 67 — `dysuria`, `nocturia`
and `urinary_frequency` — decisive accuracy *is* `true` recall, and a model that
never answers `false` is not penalised on them. That gap is the standing next
ticket and it is now blocking a real reading: we cannot tell whether Arm P's
`false` handling held up on three of six signals, because there is nothing to
measure it on.

## 6. Urinary frequency, and the pair prediction

The fifth prediction was that `nocturia` and `urinary_frequency` would resist,
being the two libraries least able to declare each other `null_on` and therefore
drawing the fewest companions from each other.

**Half held, and the half that held is the expensive one.** `nocturia` did not
resist at all: +49.3 points, mid-table, with `null` recall 13.1% → 78.3%.
`urinary_frequency` resisted on both axes at once — the only signal to miss the
20-point bar (+18.5), and the only one to pay a real detection cost (−30 points
of decisive accuracy, ~8 of 26 cells).

This is the same asymmetry the 2026-08-17 run found in the other direction. Then,
joint training resolved the near-synonym ambiguity in nocturia's favour at
urinary frequency's expense. Companions have now done it again, harder: nocturia
gets the full benefit and urinary frequency absorbs the cost. Two runs, two
mechanisms, the same signal losing.

**A model that catches 41.5% of patients reporting that they are going more
often is not usable for that question**, whatever it does for the other five.
This is the finding that most needs acting on and it is not visible anywhere in
the declared threshold.

## 7. What this run does not establish

* **The 67 are hand-written realistic submissions, written by us and labelled by
  us.** Provenance is unresolved and still gates committing the corpus
  (`arch_training.md` section 9). They are not real patient submissions and the
  wording matters for how much the headline is worth. The labeller and the model
  could share a blind spot that no statistic here would reveal.
* **Fold-mode optimism applies to every margin in this run.** Each fold's
  decision margin was selected on a sibling fold's *test* clusters, so the pooled
  numbers are flattered relative to a genuinely unseen set. This is true of Arm 0
  and Arm P alike, so it does not explain the gap between them; it does mean no
  absolute figure here is a deployment estimate.
* **67 submissions is a small sample and the per-signal slices are much smaller
  than 67.** The `null` slices carry the primary criterion and run 41–58 cells on
  five signals and 11 on `dysuria`; the decisive slices run 9 to 56. Section 5's
  table should be read as "did it collapse", which it can answer, and not as a
  ranking of the six signals, which it cannot.
* **Every prior number in this project is non-comparable** (DD16). Arm 0 is a
  regenerated control at `GENERATOR_VERSION` 3 — the `expectations.txt` split
  alone changes every `_draw_filler` outcome and therefore every generated
  example. Arm 0's own real-text figures differ substantially from the 2026-08-17
  A3 baselines and that is expected, not a discrepancy.
* **`P = 0.5` was not swept**, deliberately (DD14, DD15). It was chosen on the
  claim density of the real corpus before any real-text scoring. This run says
  0.5 works; it says nothing about whether 0.3 or 0.7 works better, and finding
  out by scoring candidates against the 67 would be selection on the holdout.
* **`--emit-signals all` (6b) is built and not measured**, per DD13.
* **Nothing here is deployable.** `EncoderOutput.validate_against` requires all
  seven keys; this is a six-head model and `recent_uti_present` has libraries and
  a fold tree but no trained head.
* **The register gap is untouched.** The 67 sit in a tidier register than the
  libraries aim at, and companions do not change register.

## 8. What follows

1. **Fix urinary frequency.** It is the one signal where this run made things
   materially worse on the thing the model is for. The first question is whether
   its `_true` library is being out-competed by the companions now sitting beside
   it — `urinary_frequency_true` was already the worst library in the 2026-08-16
   sweep at 65.8% recall, and `nocturia` is the obvious source of the confusion.
   Per-line label vectors (12.3) are what would let the two assert about each
   other properly; this run is an argument for bringing that forward.
2. **Write the missing `false` submissions.** Three of six signals have no
   explicit denial anywhere in the 67, so a third of the decisive evidence in
   section 5 does not exist. This was already the standing next ticket; it now
   blocks reading a result rather than merely narrowing one.
3. **Fold companion-bearing validation into the standard recipe regardless of
   arm.** Arm C is worth 16% for free and costs nothing but a re-selection. There
   is no reason for any future margin to be chosen on a validation split in which
   this failure cannot occur.
4. **Re-run the outstanding A1/A2/A3 sweep against version 3 or retire it.** Its
   datasets are non-comparable now and its numbers are already quoted in section
   10 as history.
5. **Do not read `81.0%` as a deployment number.** It is five fold-models, margins
   tuned on sibling test clusters, scored on 67 submissions we wrote ourselves.
   It is a very large movement in the right direction on the only instrument that
   has ever detected this failure, and that is all it is.
